GeoClaw has been engaged by Terreatek (Engesis group) to deliver a geospatial product supporting their geotechnical expertise for a landslide event at Alameda Engenheiro Gentil Forn, Jardim Gloria neighborhood, Juiz de Fora, Minas Gerais, Brazil.

3.4 Slope Stability Risk Factors

  1. Significant fill deposits (up to 5m) on hillside terrain = prior earthworks on slopes
  2. Very soft fill (N=2-5) over competent residual soil = potential slip surfaces at interface
  3. Variable depth to rock (1-20m) = irregular drainage, differential settlement risk
  4. SP13 at 20.08m = buried valley acting as groundwater collector
  5. Low current water table but seasonal variation in tropical climate is expected

4. Failure Mechanism Hypothesis

PREDISPOSING FACTORS: - Steep hillside terrain (Morro do Cristo area) - Thick fill deposits (2-5m) on natural slopes - Very soft fill material (N=2-5) over competent residual soil - Irregular bedrock creating preferential drainage paths - Buried valley at SP13 concentrating subsurface flow | v TRIGGERING FACTOR: - Intense/prolonged rainfall event - (Quantification pending - needs ERA5/CHIRPS data) | v FAILURE SEQUENCE: 1. Heavy rainfall infiltrates porous fill material 2. Perched water table develops at fill-residual soil interface 3. Pore water pressure increase reduces effective stress 4. Shear strength at contact drops below driving forces 5. Translational slide along fill-residual soil interface 6. Possible retrogressive failure expanding upslope

4.1 Evidence Supporting This Hypothesis

  • From SPT: Very soft fill (N=2-5) over competent base = classic translational slide setup
  • From SPT: Water detected in 4 boreholes even during investigation period
  • From Context: News articles reference extreme rainfall events (533mm in 6 hours) in Brazil
  • From Geology: Gneissic/granitic terrain produces lateritic soils prone to landslides
  • From InSAR (analogy): Mamring site shows displacement-rainfall correlation
